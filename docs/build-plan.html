What the client asked for

The written brief is short. The real specification is in the artifacts: a printed Victory Lane Race Standings sheet for June 2026 produced by ORION Business Strategies Group, and screenshots of a competing product ("The Comp", by Chris Collins Inc) that the client evidently uses today and wants to better.

Pulling those together, the system has to do six things.

RequirementFrom the briefWhat it implies
Rank employees, then rank stores by those rankings Verbatim Two coupled leaderboards. A store's score depends on its advisors' scores, so one scoring pass must produce both.
Manual data entry, twice weekly, noon deadline Verbatim Submission windows with a hard cutoff in a named timezone. Missing the cutoff costs the store −2 points.
Add, remove and hide employees Verbatim Hidden (PTO, sick) advisors are excluded from scoring without being deleted — history must survive.
Weighted categories, per-store monthly goals Verbatim ("see example") The example is the June sheet. Weights and goals are both data, not code. See Scoring model.
Training criteria flag Verbatim An advisor missing training loses 25 points and is flagged on the report.
Emailed reports + a message board Verbatim Standings mail to every ranked advisor and every manager on the afternoon final numbers land. Announcements live in-app with read receipts.

You noted the brief never says how users enter data. It doesn't — so the prototype covers all three plausible answers (web forms, spreadsheet upload, machine-to-machine push) behind one interface. That's Integration seams.

Scoring model Verified

The June 2026 sheet is not just a design reference — it's a complete, self-checking specification. I reverse-engineered the arithmetic and it reproduces exactly: all 8 manager rows, every advisor row I transcribed, and every team score. Nothing here is inferred loosely; each rule below was confirmed to the cent against the printed figures.

Advisor score

Per advisor, per month
pointsc  =  ( actualc ÷ goalstore,c ) × weightc
score     =  Σ pointsc  −  penalties

Weights sum to 100, so an advisor exactly on goal in every category scores 100. Attainment is not capped — the June sheet has advisors at 183% of a category goal, and Jem finished at 145.67.

Team score

Per dealership, per month
teamScore  =  mean( advisor scores at that store )

Confirmed to the cent on six dealerships — Volkswagen 105.07, Kia 101.45, Nissan SP 101.31, Chevy Cadillac 103.33, Toyota STR 101.13, Nissan 77.12. The remaining two agree to within transcription noise on the scan.

This is the coupling between the two leaderboards, and it's why hiding an advisor for PTO genuinely matters: removing one advisor moves the store's score. Which advisors count toward the mean is a scoring input, not a display filter — that decision has to be recorded and auditable.

Penalties

  • Late submission — −2 from the store score, per missed noon deadline. Applied automatically by the scheduler, not by hand.
  • Training incomplete — −25 from the advisor's score, plus a flag on the report.
  • Manual — commissioners can post an adjustment with a reason. BHoosiers' −5 in June is a multiple of the late penalty, so it is likely two-and-a-half late weeks or a discretionary call; either way the model needs a free-form penalty ledger, not just a counter.

One trap the sheet exposes

Values are printed at two decimals but scored at full precision. Quiet Storm's WC Conv prints as 0.67 and scores 6.65 — which is 0.665 exactly. Round the input to what the cell displays and the score comes out wrong, and only on this one category, because the other six carry enough resolution at their weights to survive it.

So: store raw values at full precision and round only at render. In practice numeric(12,4) holds every unit on this sheet. It's a small thing that would have been a genuinely nasty bug — a scoring discrepancy appearing on one category, for some advisors, in a system people are paid against.

This gives us a golden-master test. The June 2026 sheet is committed as a fixture with the expected points for every category: seed it, run the engine, assert the output matches the printed page to the cent. Any future change that would break a published standing fails CI. That test gets written in Phase 2, before any UI sits on top of it — and the precision trap above is exactly the kind of thing it exists to catch.

Decisions taken

Four calls I'm making so the build isn't blocked. Each is reversible, and each is flagged to the client rather than buried.

1 — Weighted goal attainment, not the incumbent's rank-sum

The screenshots show "The Comp" scoring by summing category ranks (lowest total wins). The client's own Victory Lane sheet scores by weighted attainment against goal. The brief says "the categories would be weighted" and "we will need to input monthly goal for the scoring to take place" — that's the Victory Lane model. I'm treating the screenshots as feature and UI reference, not as the scoring spec. The engine stays pluggable so rank-sum can be added as an alternative if they want both.

2 — Categories are configuration, not columns

The handwritten margin notes on the June sheet add C/P RO's YOY and Video MPI eSurveys to the manager board and Video Sent % and Video Viewed % to the advisor board. The incumbent's notifications also show a Managers Choice category whose meaning rotates monthly ("August Managers Choice will be Fuel Service"). Categories therefore change often. They live in a table with weights versioned per period, so adding one is an admin action, not a deployment.

3 — Multi-tenant and multi-league from the first migration

The incumbent has a league selector ("Asian Big Dog League") and separate prize pools per league. Retrofitting a tenant boundary later is expensive and error-prone; adding the columns now costs almost nothing. This is also what makes the enterprise story credible.

4 — Published standings are immutable snapshots

Once a month's standings are mailed, they are a record people are paid against. Late-arriving data must never silently rewrite a published board. Each scoring run stores its inputs, the engine version, and the result; corrections are issued as a new, visibly-labelled revision.

Open questions Decided

Eight things the brief left open. All eight are now decided so the build can move, and every one is implemented as a league setting or a toggle rather than a hardcoded rule — so changing any of them is a configuration change, not a rewrite. They are listed here as assumptions for the client to correct, not as blockers.

  1. 01

    What do the twice-weekly submissions contain?

    Month-to-date running totals, or that period's increment? Assuming MTD totals — the last submission of the month then is the final. Interim entries drive a provisional live leaderboard and the on-time check; only the month-end final scores.

  2. 02

    Which two days, and in which timezone?

    "Noon on that day" needs a named pair of weekdays and a zone. The incumbent's notices say PST. Assuming a per-league setting, defaulting to Mon/Thu at 12:00 America/Los_Angeles.

  3. 03

    Does the −2 late penalty stack?

    Two misses in a month — is that −2 or −4? Assuming it stacks per missed window, which is what makes BHoosiers' −5 hard to reconstruct and is worth confirming against their June records.

  4. 04

    Do the four new handwritten categories add or replace?

    The note beside Team Score reads "4th category — Video MPI eSurveys", but there are already four manager categories. If these are additions, all weights need restating so each board still totals 100.

  5. 05

    When an advisor is hidden mid-month, what happens to the store?

    Excluded from the team-score mean entirely, or included at their partial numbers? Assuming full exclusion for any month they're hidden, matching the incumbent's rule that two-plus weeks off the drive removes them.

  6. 06

    Which of the incumbent's eligibility rules carry over?

    Their rulebook has a 60-day new-hire grace period, a floater-advisor rule, and a minimum of two advisors for a manager to be eligible to win. Sensible rules; not in the brief. Assuming they carry over, implemented as toggles.

  7. 07

    Who is allowed to enter data for a store?

    Manager only, or any delegate? This drives the role model and the audit trail. Assuming the manager plus named delegates, with every write attributed.

  8. 08

    Which sending domain, and does verification stay manual?

    The prototype mails from a subdomain of ethandbard.com. Production needs the client's domain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC. Separately, their rules require the monthly winner to submit DMS reports as proof — assuming that stays a human process, with the platform only storing the attachments.

Two things worth designing deliberately

Scheduled jobs must be idempotent. This app's scheduler sends payroll-adjacent email and applies point penalties. A duplicate container or a redeploy that fires the same job twice is not a cosmetic bug — it double-penalises a store or mails two conflicting standings. Every scheduled run takes a Postgres advisory lock, and every send carries an idempotency key of (template, period, recipient) that the mail log enforces.

Auth is behind an interface from day one. The prototype uses app-native sessions with emailed magic links — no passwords to store, and the brief already requires an email on every profile. For an enterprise deployment the same interface accepts a trusted identity header from Cloudflare Access, so the app moves onto corporate SSO without touching route code.

The participation table is the one that's easy to get wrong. "Hide this advisor" reads like a UI toggle, but because team score is a mean, hiding somebody changes their manager's score and therefore the store's rank. It belongs in the scored record with an author and a timestamp, not in a boolean on the employee row.

The four surfaces

Enter

A per-store grid for the current window: one row per advisor, one column per category, last-saved timestamp on each row — the shape the incumbent already trained these managers on. Above it, the store-level fields for the manager board. A countdown to the cutoff, and an explicit warning that submitting after noon costs two points. Paste-from-spreadsheet works on the grid, since these numbers come out of a DMS report today.

Manage

Roster (add, remove, hide with a reason, restore), monthly goals with carry-forward from last month so nobody retypes 56 numbers, category and weight editing with a live "must total 100" check, the training-criteria flag, and the penalty ledger. Every screen writes to the audit log.

See

The Victory Lane board, faithful to the printed sheet — position colour running green to red, best and worst in each category highlighted, both tables on one page — because that artifact is what the client already circulates, and matching it is how the tool earns trust in week one. It prints to a single page. Alongside it: an advisor's personal card showing their categories, their gap to the next position, and what one more of each unit would be worth; a manager's store view; and trend charts across periods.

Communicate

Templated mail for the monthly standings, the pre-deadline reminder, the late-penalty notice, the training flag, and announcements. Scheduled per league, editable in the admin UI, and every template previewable against real data before it sends. Administrators can also trigger any of them on demand — with a recipient count and a confirmation step, because "send to all 53 people" deserves one.

Integration seams

You asked that the prototype be adaptable to an enterprise network without knowing what the client's internal tooling is. The way to buy that option cheaply is to make manual entry one implementation of an interface rather than the only path in.

One interface, four implementations
MetricSource.submit( period, store, rows[] ) → ValidationResult

Every path lands in the same validator, the same audit log and the same submission record. A number pushed by a machine is indistinguishable downstream from one typed by a manager, except in its provenance field.

  • Manual entry — the web grid. The baseline, and the only one the prototype needs to be useful.
  • File import — CSV or XLSX with column mapping, a validation preview, and a diff against what's already filed. This is the realistic bridge, because these figures live in DMS exports today.
  • REST API — scoped keys per store. What an internal script or an RPA job would call.
  • MCP server — the same write operations exposed as tools, so the client's own agents can file numbers, query standings, or post announcements. Worth building in the prototype precisely because it is cheap once the interface exists, and it's the concrete answer when they ask whether this fits their AI tooling.

A DmsAdapter stub sits alongside these with the shape a Reynolds & Reynolds or CDK pull would take. It stays unimplemented until somebody can tell us what they actually run — but its presence makes the integration conversation concrete instead of hypothetical.

Ships → a live URL and a demo

Phases 1 and 2 are the ones worth not rushing. After Phase 2 the risk profile drops sharply, because everything remaining is interface work over a proven core. A useful demo exists at the end of Phase 5; the system is genuinely operable at the end of Phase 6.

Out of scope for the prototype

  • Cash prize disbursement. The incumbent's rules pay out $250 / $100 / $50 / $35 / $25 and restrict prizes outside the US. The platform records winners and prize tiers; it does not move money.
  • Live DMS integration. Adapter shape only, until somebody names the system.
  • Native mobile apps. The web UI is responsive; managers will file from a desk.
  • Historical backfill. June 2026 loads as a fixture. Importing prior seasons is a separate conversation once the import path exists.
  • The client's own domain and branding. The prototype lives on a subdomain of ethandbard.com under a racing-themed identity derived from their existing sheet.
